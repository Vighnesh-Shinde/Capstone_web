1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================

Depression is common, treatable, and badly under-detected. In a first counselling
conversation the counsellor has only their own impression to work from: there is no
instrument that measures what was said and how it was said, and no record of the
evidence behind that impression. Self-report questionnaires (PHQ-8/PHQ-9) depend on the
person answering accurately in the moment, and are easy to under-answer.

Formal problem statement:

  "Given an audio-video recording of a counselling conversation, produce an
   evidence-backed screening indicator of depression for the participant only,
   which (a) separates the participant's speech from everyone else's, (b) measures
   what they said, how they said it, and how their face moved, (c) combines those
   measurements into a single score with a stated decision cut-off, (d) explains that
   score feature by feature in language a non-technical reader can act on, and
   (e) refuses to answer when the recording is outside the range the models were
   trained on - all inside a secured, consent-governed web platform that a counsellor
   and an administrator can operate without a developer."

The last two clauses are what separate this from a notebook experiment: an unexplained
number is not usable in mental health, and a confident number produced from unusable
input is actively dangerous.

3. THE DATASET: DAIC-WOZ
================================================================================

3.1 What it is
  DAIC-WOZ = Distress Analysis Interview Corpus - Wizard of Oz, University of Southern
  California (Gratch et al., LREC 2014). Clinical-style interviews conducted by an
  on-screen virtual interviewer called "Ellie", operated by a human in another room
  (the "wizard"). It is the benchmark corpus of the AVEC 2016 and AVEC 2017 depression
  sub-challenges. Access requires an approved request; ours was approved. The corpus may
  NOT be redistributed, which is why it is kept out of the git repository.

3.2 What each participant folder contains
  - AUDIO.wav                 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM, whole session
  - TRANSCRIPT.csv            start_time, stop_time, speaker, value (one row per utterance)
  - COVAREP.csv / FORMANT.csv pre-computed audio features at 100 Hz (not used by us)
  - CLNF_features/_features3D 68 facial landmarks per frame (30 fps)
  - CLNF_AUs.txt              facial action-unit intensities and presence flags
  - CLNF_gaze.txt             gaze direction, in camera frame AND head frame
  - CLNF_pose.txt             head translation and rotation
  - CLNF_hog.bin              appearance descriptors (not used by us)

  NOTE: DAIC-WOZ ships NO raw video. This single fact decides several design choices
  later in this document - most importantly, Grad-CAM is impossible (section 18.4).

3.3 Cohort actually used (measured in our own Phase-1 audit)
  - Participants listed in the official split files ..... 189
  - Folders present on disk ............................. 188
  - Excluded: participant 440 ........................... corrupt in the official release
  - Release-documented exclusions (already absent) ...... 342, 394, 398, 460
  - USED ................................................ 188
  - Depressed 55 (29.3%) / Not depressed 133 (70.7%)
  - PHQ-8 score: min 0, median 5, mean 6.68, max 23
  - One label anomaly: one participant has PHQ8_Score = 10 but PHQ8_Binary = 0. We kept
    the released label rather than "fixing" it, and reported the anomaly.

3.4 Participant speech volume (annotation tokens stripped) - this defines our guards
                        min     5th pct    median      max
    utterance rows       42        76       150.5      385
    words               165       489      1247.5     4551
    duration (minutes)   6.9      10.0       15.2      32.8
  Three transcripts contain no interviewer rows at all.

3.5 LABELLING - two different labels, never mixed
  (a) TRAINING LABEL (DAIC-WOZ): PHQ8_Binary. The 8-item Patient Health Questionnaire
      total (0-24) thresholded at >= 10, the standard cut-off for probable depression.
      It is a questionnaire outcome, not a psychiatrist's diagnosis - an important
      honesty point in the viva.
  (b) PLATFORM LABEL (our own research dataset): the COUNSELLOR'S OWN ASSESSMENT,
      recorded on the report. The model's prediction is NEVER used as a label, because
      training on your own output teaches nothing and launders error into "truth".
  The two live in separate database tables and are never averaged together.

3.6 Official split
  train 107 - dev 34 - test 47 participants, participant-disjoint (nobody appears in two
  splits). Class balance is similar across splits (about 28-30% depressed).

================================================================================
4. WHY THIS CORPUS IS HARDER THAN THE LITERATURE SUGGESTS
================================================================================

Burdisso et al. (ClinicalNLP @ NAACL 2024) showed that a model reading ONLY the
interviewer's prompts reaches about 0.90 F1 on DAIC-WOZ - the highest text-only result
reported on the corpus - because the interview branches on the participant's answers, so
which questions Ellie asked leaks the outcome. Any model trained on the full transcript
is partly exploiting that, and the leak disappears in real deployment.

Rules we adopted as a result, all of them costly:
  - Train on the PARTICIPANT'S SPEECH ONLY. Interviewer turns are dropped everywhere.
  - Remove interviewer-dependent audio timing. 24 of the original 85 audio features
    measured response latency and between-turn gaps - that is, Ellie's scripted pacing.
    Removing them costs accuracy (audio-only cross-validated AUC 0.671 -> 0.609) but
    what remains is genuinely the participant's voice.
  - Never use gender. The training split has an 18-point depression-rate gap between
    genders that does not exist in dev or test: a shortcut that would inflate our
    numbers and then fail on real people.

This is why our numbers look lower than some published ones, and why we can defend them.

================================================================================
5. FEATURES: WHAT THE COMPUTER ACTUALLY MEASURES
================================================================================

5.1 TEXT - 3,096 numbers per session
  (a) 24 lexical features, counted from the participant's cleaned words:
      total words; number of answers; words per answer (mean/std/median); type-token
      ratio; root type-token ratio; mean word length; rates of first-person singular
      ("I, me, my"), first-person plural, second person, third person; absolutist words
      ("always, never, nothing"); negative-emotion words; positive-emotion words;
      negations; hedges ("maybe", "I guess"); past-tense words; sleep/fatigue words;
      self-focus ratio; negative-to-positive ratio; sentiment balance; very-short-answer
      rate; long-answer rate.
      These are research-backed markers (self-focus, absolutist language, negative
      affect) and, crucially, a human can read them.
  (b) 3,072 sentence-meaning features: every answer is embedded with
      sentence-transformers/all-mpnet-base-v2 (768 dimensions per sentence, maximum 64
      tokens, float32), then pooled four ways and concatenated:
          [ mean | length-weighted mean | max | standard deviation ] x 768 = 3,072
      The max block matters clinically: one very dark sentence in an otherwise flat
      interview is exactly what an average would erase.
  Block order is fixed and is part of the model contract. Getting it wrong produces a
  confident, wrong answer that nothing downstream can detect.

5.2 AUDIO - 64 numbers per session (the "clean-64" set)
  Computed with librosa over participant speech only:
      - utterance duration and words-per-utterance, each summarised by 9 functionals
        (mean, std, p10, p25, p50, p75, p90, inter-quartile range, slope)
      - total speech time, number of utterances, total words, words per second
      - loudness: RMS in dB, 9 functionals plus range
      - voiced ratio
      - pitch: YIN estimator, 65-400 Hz, converted to semitones, 9 functionals plus range
      - jitter proxy (frame-to-frame pitch change) and shimmer proxy (frame-to-frame
        loudness change), 9 functionals each
      - three participant-only timing features re-derived so they never reference the
        interviewer: session_len_s_pt, speech_ratio_pt, utts_per_minute_pt
  Clinical grounding: flattened pitch, reduced loudness variation and slowed speech are
  documented correlates of depression (psychomotor retardation).

5.3 VIDEO - 224 numbers per session (OpenFace)
  From OpenFace 2.2 using the CLNF landmark model, the same family DAIC-WOZ used:
      - 14 action-unit intensities (AU01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 09, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17, 20,
        25, 26), each summarised by 8 statistics - mean, std, p10, p50, p90, max, and
        two movement statistics (mean and std of frame-to-frame change) = 112
      - 6 action-unit presence rates (AU04, 12, 15, 23, 28, 45) = 6
      - au_total, the sum of the 14 intensities, with the same 8 statistics = 8
      - gaze: 12 columns (both eyes; camera-frame x/y/z and head-frame x/y/z), 8
        statistics each = 96
      - au_frac_tracked and gz_frac_tracked, how much of the session was trackable = 2
      TOTAL = 224
  Frames are filtered to success == 1 and confidence >= 0.75, and every recording is
  resampled to 30 fps first, because the movement statistics scale with frame rate.
  Separately, the platform also stores 111 MediaPipe face-geometry features for its own
  future research dataset; those are not used by the DAIC-WOZ models.

  What an Action Unit is: the Facial Action Coding System describes facial movement as
  individual muscle actions. AU04 is the brow lowerer (frowning), AU12 the lip-corner
  puller (smiling), AU15 the lip-corner depressor, AU17 the chin raiser, AU45 a blink.

================================================================================
6. FROM RECORDING TO FEATURES: THE INFERENCE PIPELINE
================================================================================

  1. The counsellor uploads a session recording (common formats, up to 520 MB).
  2. ffmpeg extracts 16 kHz mono audio.
  3. faster-whisper (model "small", int8 quantised, CPU) transcribes with word-level
     timestamps.
  4. pyannote.audio (speaker-diarization-community-1) decides who spoke when.
  5. SPEAKER IDENTIFICATION by voiceprint. The counsellor - and any companion in the
     room - enrolled earlier by reading a fixed passage, giving a 256-dimensional voice
     embedding. Each diarized speaker is compared to those by cosine similarity. A
     speaker is claimed by a known voice if similarity >= 0.55 AND it beats the
     runner-up by >= 0.06. Whoever is left unclaimed is the participant. If that is
     ambiguous - no one left over, or more than one unknown voice - the session is
     REFUSED (SPEAKER_UNVERIFIED) rather than scoring the wrong person.
  6. ADEQUACY GUARD. If the participant produced fewer than 165 words or fewer than 39
     speaker turns, the session is refused as TOO_SHORT. Section 7.5 explains the real
     bug that made this necessary.
  7. FEATURE EXTRACTION, participant speech only: text 3,096; audio 64; video 224 via
     OpenFace, which runs as a separate process IN PARALLEL with transcription so it
     adds almost nothing to the wall-clock time.
  8. Each model is fed BY COLUMN NAME from its own stored list of column names, so a
     model can never silently receive the wrong feature set.
  9. Scoring (section 7), explanation (section 18), report stored, and the recording
     purged on the retention schedule.

================================================================================
7. THE MODELS, AND HOW THEY WERE TRAINED
================================================================================

Three generations of model exist in this project. All of them are trained on the same
188 DAIC-WOZ participants with the same rules; they differ in how the modalities are
combined.

7.1 The building block: one scikit-learn Pipeline per modality (LATE FUSION)
      SimpleImputer(median)  ->  StandardScaler  ->  SelectKBest(ANOVA F)  ->  LogisticRegression
    - SimpleImputer(median): a feature that could not be measured arrives as NaN (never
      as 0, which would be a real measurement of "none") and is filled with the median
      of the training data.
    - StandardScaler: each feature becomes (x - mean) / std, so features with different
      units are comparable.
    - SelectKBest: keeps the k features most associated with the label by ANOVA F-test.
      With 188 people and 3,096 text features, selection is not optional - without it a
      model memorises the training split.
    - LogisticRegression: a linear model, chosen deliberately so every prediction can be
      explained exactly (section 18).

7.2 GENERATION 1 - LATE FUSION (three models plus a combiner)
    text  : SelectKBest k=50,  C=0.01   -> test AUC 0.7532
    audio : SelectKBest k=25,  C=1.0    -> test AUC 0.6017
    video : SelectKBest k=25,  C=1.0    -> test AUC 0.5844
    fusion: LogisticRegression over the three probabilities [p_text, p_audio, p_video]
            trained on OUT-OF-FOLD probabilities (see 7.6)
            coefficients: text +1.988, audio +0.909, video -0.510, intercept -1.633
            threshold 0.2945
            test: AUC 0.7684, accuracy 0.7021, F1 0.6111, sensitivity 0.786, specificity 0.667
    Reading of those coefficients: the combiner learned to trust text most, audio less,
    and to treat the video probability as mildly NEGATIVE evidence - on this corpus the
    video model was at or below chance, so the combiner used it as a correction, not as
    support.

7.3 GENERATION 2 - EARLY FUSION v1 (one model on everything, joined)
    All 3,384 features (3,096 text + 64 audio + 224 video) are concatenated into ONE
    input vector, and one model is trained on it:
      ColumnTransformer(
          text    -> Imputer -> Scaler -> PCA(40)
          audio64 -> Imputer -> Scaler -> PCA(10)
          video   -> Imputer -> Scaler -> PCA(10) )   ->  LogisticRegression(elastic-net)
    Why PCA per block rather than one SelectKBest over all 3,384 columns: we tested the
    naive version and it reproduced the classic early-fusion failure exactly - a global
    SelectKBest chose 50 text columns and ZERO audio or video columns, so "early fusion"
    returned byte-identical numbers to a text-only model. That is demonstrated in our
    results, not asserted.
    Outcome: official-test AUC 0.8333, accuracy 0.766, F1 0.667 - but the elastic-net
    penalty (which can drive coefficients to exactly zero) had deleted the ENTIRE audio
    block. Weight shares came out text 97-98%, audio 0.0%, video 2-3%. It was
    multimodal in name only.

7.4 GENERATION 3 - EARLY FUSION v2 (the model this project ships)
    Two changes fix the previous problem:
    (a) L2 penalty only. L2 shrinks coefficients but never sets them exactly to zero, so
        no block can be deleted. (L1 / elastic-net is what deleted audio in v1.)
    (b) Block balancing. Each block's PCA output is multiplied by a weight w_b / sqrt(k_b)
        using ColumnTransformer(transformer_weights=...). Without this, the text block
        dominates by construction simply because it has more columns and more components.
        The weights were tuned inside cross-validation.
    Final shipped configuration:
        text    3,096 columns -> PCA 20 components, block weight 0.2236
        audio64    64 columns -> PCA 10 components, block weight 0.6325
        video     224 columns -> PCA 10 components, block weight 0.3162
        classifier: LogisticRegression(penalty='l2', C=0.1) over the 40 components
        decision threshold 0.4412 (0.3449 when refitted under the official protocol)
    Acceptance rule - stated openly as a PRODUCT REQUIREMENT, not a data finding:
        text share must be the largest, audio >= 10%, video >= 5% of the back-mapped
        coefficient mass. 90 of the 384 candidate configurations met it; the best of
        those by cross-validated AUC was shipped.
    Achieved shares: text 77.1%, audio 12.8%, video 10.2%.
    THE COST, measured and reported rather than hidden:
        cross-validated AUC 0.7633 (unconstrained) -> 0.7056 (constrained) = -0.0577
        seed-42 test AUC     0.8249 -> 0.7946
    In other words: forcing audio and video to count makes the model slightly worse on
    this corpus. We ship the constrained model because the project requires a genuinely
    multimodal decision, and we say so.

7.5 THE DECISION THRESHOLD, and the bug that taught us to guard the input
    A classifier outputs a probability; a threshold turns it into a decision. We do NOT
    use 0.5, because only ~30% of the training participants are depressed - at 0.5 the
    model would miss most of them. The threshold is chosen by YOUDEN'S J STATISTIC:
        J = sensitivity + specificity - 1, maximised over candidate thresholds,
        computed on OUT-OF-FOLD predictions of the training data only - never on test.
    The bug that this project is most careful about: in an early version, every SHORT
    recording came back "depressed". The cause was not the threshold but the input: a
    one-minute recording is far outside the training range, the text model's RBF kernel
    collapsed to a constant 0.2542 for every such session, and 0.2542 happened to sit
    just above the then-threshold of 0.48 after fusion. A model fed data unlike its
    training data still returns a confident number. Hence the adequacy guard (165 words /
    39 turns) and the out-of-range warnings on every report.

7.6 EVALUATION PROTOCOL (the part that makes the numbers trustworthy)
    - PRIMARY: 10 repeated stratified 80/20 participant splits. Inside each 80%,
      5-fold cross-validation chooses PCA sizes, block weights, C and the threshold; the
      20% is scored once. Crucially the WHOLE selection is redone inside every split, so
      the mean is not inflated by choosing a winner on one lucky split. We learned this
      the hard way: on a single split, early fusion looked like a clear winner (0.7736 vs
      0.7343); across 10 splits that reversed.
    - SECONDARY: the official AVEC-2017 protocol - train on train+dev (141 people), score
      the official 47-person test split ONCE. This is the number comparable with
      published work.
    - Confidence intervals: 10,000-sample bootstrap over test participants.
    - Out-of-fold probabilities: for the late-fusion combiner, the three model
      probabilities are produced by cross_val_predict so the combiner never sees
      predictions made on data the base models trained on. Otherwise it would learn from
      over-confident inputs and set the wrong weights.

8. EARLY FUSION vs LATE FUSION (the concept your mentor will ask about)
================================================================================

LATE FUSION (decision-level): train one model per modality, then combine their
OUTPUT PROBABILITIES.
    + Each modality can use the algorithm that suits it; missing modalities are easy to
      handle; the combiner's weights are directly interpretable.
    - The modalities never interact: any relationship between "flat voice" AND "negative
      words" is invisible, because each model has already collapsed to one number.

EARLY FUSION (feature-level): concatenate all features into ONE vector and train ONE
model on it.
    + The model can use relationships ACROSS modalities, because it sees all the raw
      measurements together.
    - Scale and width imbalance destroys it if handled naively: 3,096 text columns next
      to 64 audio columns means the selection or the regularisation simply throws the
      small blocks away. That is exactly what we measured in v1.
  Our solution - the standard one in the literature and the one we implemented:
    per-block dimensionality reduction (PCA) so each modality contributes a comparable
    number of components, plus per-block weights so no modality dominates by width, and
    an L2 penalty so no block can be zeroed.

INTERMEDIATE / HYBRID FUSION exists too (e.g. gated or attention-based fusion at
utterance level). The earlier research this project inherited tried six such
architectures - BiLSTM, transformer, cross-attention, gated and bilinear fusion - and all
of them lost to simple models on this corpus, because 107-142 training people is far too
few to fit them honestly.

================================================================================
9. THE ARITHMETIC, END TO END (worked, with real numbers)
================================================================================

Everything below is the shipped v2 early-fusion model.

STEP 1 - Impute. Any unmeasured feature becomes the training median.

STEP 2 - Standardise. For each column i:
             z_i = (x_i - mean_i) / std_i
         mean_i and std_i are the training values, stored in the model bundle
         (feature_means, feature_stds). Note: for a column with zero variance,
         StandardScaler stores scale_ = 1.0, NOT 0.0 - dividing by the raw std there
         would be division by zero. (The GPU run caught and fixed exactly this.)

STEP 3 - PCA per block. For block b with k_b components:
             c_b = (z_b - pca_mean_b) @ components_b^T           (shape: k_b numbers)
         text -> 20 components, audio -> 10, video -> 10. Total 40.

STEP 4 - Block weighting. Each block's components are multiplied by its weight:
             text x 0.2236,  audio x 0.6325,  video x 0.3162
         (each weight is w_b / sqrt(k_b), so wide blocks do not win by width alone).

STEP 5 - Logistic regression over the 40 weighted components:
             z (the log-odds) = intercept + sum_j coef_j * component_j
         intercept = -1.2749 in the shipped model.

STEP 6 - Probability (the percentage on the report):
             p = 1 / (1 + e^(-z))                     [the sigmoid / logistic function]

STEP 7 - Decision:
             p >= threshold (0.4412)  ->  "Depressed"
             p <  threshold           ->  "Not depressed"

WORKED EXAMPLE (verification participant 458, from the GPU run):
             sum of all SHAP values + intercept = z = -6.7561
             p = 1 / (1 + e^(6.7561)) = 0.00116  = 0.1%
             0.001 < 0.4412  ->  Not depressed.   True label: Not depressed. Correct.

SECOND EXAMPLE (participant 384):
             z = +0.2713  ->  p = 1 / (1 + e^(-0.2713)) = 0.5674 = 56.7%
             0.567 >= 0.4412  ->  Depressed.      True label: Depressed. Correct.

BACK-MAPPING A COEFFICIENT TO ONE ORIGINAL FEATURE (this is what makes explanation
possible at all). PCA is a linear map, so the chain scaler -> PCA -> linear model is
itself linear in the original columns:
             w_original = (coef[block's components] * block_weight) @ components_b
         giving one weight per original column. The block weight MUST be included; if it
         is left out every contribution and every modality share is silently wrong. (We
         found and fixed exactly this in the website's code when v2 arrived.)

SHAP VALUE OF FEATURE i (exact, no sampling):
             shap_i = w_original_i * (x_i - mean_i) / std_i
         and the additivity property holds exactly:
             sum_i shap_i + intercept = z = the model's log-odds
         Verified on 5 held-out participants: maximum difference 3.553e-15, which is
         floating-point noise. Cross-checked against the shap library's LinearExplainer:
         maximum per-feature difference 6.193e-16.

MODALITY SHARE (what the report shows as "text 77%, audio 13%, video 10%"):
             share_m = sum over features in m of |shap_i|  /  sum over ALL features of |shap_i|
         It answers "how much of the model's reasoning rested on this modality", which is
         NOT the same as "this modality's own verdict" - a single early-fusion model has
         no per-modality verdict, and the report says so.

THRESHOLD SELECTION (Youden's J):
             J(t) = sensitivity(t) + specificity(t) - 1, maximised over t,
         computed on out-of-fold training predictions only.

CONFUSION MATRIX AND THE METRICS (official protocol, shipped v2 model):
             TN = 24   FP = 9
             FN = 2    TP = 12
             accuracy    = (TP+TN)/total = 36/47 = 0.766
             sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN)    = 12/14 = 0.857   (depressed people found)
             specificity = TN/(TN+FP)    = 24/33 = 0.727   (non-depressed cleared)
             precision   = TP/(TP+FP)    = 12/21 = 0.571
             F1          = 2*P*R/(P+R)   = 0.6857
             balanced accuracy = (0.857+0.727)/2 = 0.792
             ROC-AUC     = 0.8463

================================================================================
10. WHY THE OUTPUT IS A PERCENTAGE, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
================================================================================

The number on the report is the model's DEPRESSION SCORE: the probability it assigns to
the "depressed" class, from the sigmoid in step 6. Three things to be clear about:

  - It is NOT a severity. 80% does not mean "more depressed" than 60%; it means the
    pattern resembles the depressed group more strongly.
  - It is NOT the model's confidence in its own correctness.
  - It is compared against a CUT-OFF THAT IS NOT 50%. For the shipped model the cut-off
    is 44% (and 34% under the official-protocol refit; the v1 late-fusion model used
    29%). A score of 44% therefore means "Depressed". This confused a real reader during
    development - a report read "Depressed, 35% confidence" - which is why the report now
    labels the number "depression score", draws the cut-off as a tick on the ring, and
    explains in one sentence why the cut-off is below half.

Why the cut-off is below 50%: only about 3 in 10 people in the training data were
depressed, so the model's probabilities sit low overall; a 0.5 cut-off would miss most
depressed participants. The threshold is set where sensitivity and specificity are
jointly best (Youden's J).

================================================================================
11. GUARDS: WHEN THE SYSTEM REFUSES TO ANSWER
================================================================================

  TOO_SHORT            participant produced < 165 words or < 39 speaker turns.
                       Measured floors from the training split. A shorter interview is
                       outside the range every model was fitted on, and the failure mode
                       is not "slightly worse" but "confidently wrong in one direction".
  SPEAKER_UNVERIFIED   the participant could not be identified unambiguously - the
                       counsellor's voice was not found, nobody was left over, or more
                       than one unknown voice was present.
  VIDEO_UNAVAILABLE    the serving model needs facial measurements and no face could be
                       tracked. Substituting a neutral value would silently change the
                       arithmetic, so the session is refused instead.
  FAILED               anything else (ML service unreachable, unreadable media), with the
                       reason stored on the session.
  OUT-OF-RANGE WARNING (not a refusal) every session also reports how many of the
                       features the model uses sit more than 4 standard deviations from
                       the training data, and names the worst one. This is printed on the
                       report so a counsellor knows when to discount the score. Typical
                       causes: microphone level, camera and lighting, interview length.

12. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

Three services, each doing what it is good at:

   BROWSER (React 19 + Vite)
        |  REST + JWT (Bearer token)
        v
   BACKEND (Spring Boot 3.3.4, Java 17)  <---- JDBC ---->  PostgreSQL 16 (Docker, port 5433)
        |  internal REST, shared-secret token
        v
   ML SERVICE (Python 3.11, FastAPI)  ---->  model files (.joblib), ffmpeg, Whisper,
                                              pyannote, OpenFace, MediaPipe

Why three services rather than one:
  - The ML ecosystem is Python; the business application (security, transactions,
    relational data) is far better served by Spring Boot. Forcing either into the other's
    language would be a permanent tax.
  - The ML service can be restarted, scaled or kept OFF the public internet independently.
    It is the component that loads .joblib files, and loading a .joblib executes code, so
    isolating it is a security decision as much as an engineering one.
  - Models can be replaced without touching or redeploying the Java code.

12.1 HOW THE DESIGN WAS ARRIVED AT (the process, not just the result)
  1. Contract first. The ML service's response shape was fixed before any real model
     existed, and a mock pipeline returned that exact shape. That is why swapping in real
     models, then a three-input fusion model, then an early-fusion model never required a
     change to the backend or the frontend contract.
  2. Database before features. Sessions, consent and reports were modelled first; feature
     vectors were added later as JSONB precisely so a model with a different width would
     never force a schema migration.
  3. Refusals designed in, not bolted on. Each guard (too short, unidentified speaker,
     missing face) was added the moment the failure was understood, and each one returns a
     status the counsellor can act on rather than a stack trace.
  4. One stylesheet, tokens not components. A single design system (violet accent, warm
     neutral ground, red reserved for real errors) with reusable loading/empty/error
     states, so 29 pages look like one product and a new page inherits the behaviour.
  5. Explanation treated as a feature, not a report footer. When the text model became
     linear, the report gained per-feature reasons; when sentence vectors became
     available, it gained per-sentence reasons; when OpenFace arrived, per-region ones.
  6. Every change verified against the corpus rather than assumed - which is how the gaze
     bug and the constant-output bug were both caught.

Size of the system as built: about 24,700 lines across the three services - 29 frontend
pages, 33 frontend components, 19 REST controllers, 31 backend services, 27 JPA entities,
15 Flyway migrations, 12 ML pipeline modules, 37 git commits.

  MODEL MANAGEMENT (the "no developer needed" part)
    1. Admin uploads a .joblib on Admin -> Models.
    2. The backend stores it, hashes it (SHA-256) and asks the ML service to VALIDATE it:
       is it a dict with a model and a threshold, does it expose predict_proba, and is
       every column it names something this platform actually measures? Validation is by
       COLUMN NAME, not by counting columns - two different 224-column feature sets look
       identical to a width check.
    3. A rejected file is deleted and never recorded.
    4. Activating a version writes models/active_manifest.json and triggers a hot reload;
       the cache is only swapped once every model loads successfully.
    5. "Revert to built-in" restores the shipped weights (text/audio/fusion) or removes
       the model entirely (video, early fusion - there is no built-in for those).
    6. Rules enforced: a three-input fusion model cannot be activated without an active
       video model, and a video model cannot be removed while one depends on it.
    SECURITY NOTE: a .joblib is a Python pickle and loading one runs code. Upload is
    ADMIN-only, the internal endpoint needs the shared secret, and the ML service must
    never be exposed to the internet.

18. EXPLAINABLE AI (XAI) IN THIS PROJECT
================================================================================

18.1 Why the models are linear on purpose
  Every shipped model is linear (logistic regression, with PCA which is also linear).
  That is a deliberate trade: a non-linear model might score slightly higher, but its
  prediction could only be explained by approximation. With a linear chain, the exact
  contribution of every feature can be computed in closed form - no sampling, no
  surrogate model, no "explanation" that is itself a guess.

18.2 SHAP - for text and audio (and video too, in fact)
  SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) assigns each feature the share of the prediction
  it is responsible for, with the guarantee that the shares add up to the prediction.
  For a linear model the Shapley value has a closed form:
        shap_i = w_original_i * (x_i - mean_i) / std_i
  which is what our service computes, using the weights back-mapped through the PCA
  (section 9). Properties we verified on the GPU machine and re-verified on the website:
     - additivity: sum(shap) + intercept = the model's log-odds, max error 3.553e-15
     - agreement with the shap library's LinearExplainer: max error 6.193e-16
  So when the report says "used more negative words: +0.31", that number is the exact
  amount that feature moved the log-odds, not an approximation.
  NOTE for anyone re-running the check: shap's default masker subsamples the background
  to 100 rows and produces a spurious ~1e-3 disagreement; build the masker explicitly.

18.3 Sentence-level attribution - the text explanation a human can act on
  A SHAP value for "mpnet_734" is useless to a counsellor. But the text features are
  POOLED sentence embeddings, so the text score can be split back across the
  participant's own sentences, exactly:
        mean block          sentence s contributes  w . v_s / n
        weighted-mean block                          w . (weight_s * v_s)
        max block           each dimension's contribution belongs to the sentence that
                            achieved the maximum in that dimension
        std block           NOT linear in a single sentence - reported as an
                            "unattributed remainder", never spread around to make the
                            totals look complete
  Verified: sentence totals + remainder reconstruct the text SHAP total to 5.55e-15.
  On the report this becomes "Which answers moved the result", quoting the participant's
  own sentences with a bar showing how strongly each pushed, and in which direction.

18.4 GRAD-CAM: why it is NOT used, and what replaces it
  Grad-CAM produces a heat map by taking the gradient of the class score with respect to
  the activations of a CONVOLUTIONAL feature map and projecting it back onto the INPUT
  IMAGE. It needs three things: a convolutional network, spatial feature maps, and the
  image itself at inference time.
  This project has none of them:
     - No convolutional network. The video path is StandardScaler -> PCA -> logistic
       regression over tabular numbers. The gradient of the output with respect to the
       input is just the model's constant coefficient vector - identical for every
       session, so a "heat map" from it would carry no session-specific information.
     - No spatial dimension. The 224 video features are per-session STATISTICS (mean,
       std, percentiles, frame-to-frame change) of action units and gaze. A statistic
       over a whole interview has no (x, y) location to shade.
     - No frames. DAIC-WOZ ships no raw video, so no CNN could be trained on it, and the
       platform deletes the recording after processing, so there is no image to overlay.
  WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED to make Grad-CAM legitimate here: a consented raw-video dataset,
  a CNN trained on face crops with enough participants to fit it honestly, and evidence
  that such a model beats the action-unit features at all (video has scored at or below
  chance on this corpus in three separate runs).
  WHAT WE DELIVER INSTEAD - and label honestly: a FACE-REGION ATTRIBUTION. Every one of
  the 224 video features is mapped to the part of the face it measures (brows, eyes,
  cheeks, nose, lips, mouth corners, mouth opening, jaw, chin, gaze direction, overall
  movement, tracking quality). The report shades a face diagram by each region's real
  SHAP contribution. It is a genuine per-region attribution over what was measured, and
  the report calls it "which facial measurements the model weighed" - never Grad-CAM,
  and never implied to be a pixel saliency map.

18.5 What the counsellor actually sees on a report
  1. Depression score as a ring, with the decision cut-off marked on it.
  2. A caution box when measurements fall far outside the training range, naming the
     worst one in plain words (for example "Speaking volume").
  3. Modality contributions - text / audio / video shares, with a sentence explaining
     that for a single fused model these are shares of the reasoning, not separate verdicts.
  4. Explainability breakdown - the strongest features, each with what was observed and
     which way it pushed the result, translated out of model jargon by a glossary.
  5. "Which answers moved the result" - the participant's own sentences.
  6. "What the face contributed" - the shaded face diagram and region list.
  7. "In plain words" - the same evidence written to be read aloud to the participant,
     including the error rate and an explicit statement that this is not a diagnosis.
  8. The counsellor's own assessment, stored separately and never fed back to the model.

  Share of the decision (back-mapped coefficient mass):  text 77.1%  audio 12.8%  video 10.2%

      without audio  0.7247   (+0.0191)
      without video  0.7569   (+0.0512)
  Honest reading: text carries the signal. Audio and video are included because the
  project requires a multimodal decision, and their inclusion costs a little accuracy on
  this corpus. We report the cost rather than implying they help.

19.5 The cost of forcing multimodality (v2)
      cross-validated AUC 0.7633 -> 0.7056   (-0.0577)

19.6 Verification that the website computes the same thing as the training machine
  - The website's text extractor reproduces the training features on the corpus:
    24 lexical features exact to 3.55e-15; sentence vectors within 1e-3 (GPU half
    precision in v1; v2 fixed this by disabling TF32, giving parity at 6.4e-08).
  - Participant timing features match to 1e-13 across all 188 participants.
  - Running the training models on the corpus through the website's own code reproduces
    the published AUCs exactly: text 0.7532, audio 0.6017.
  - Early-fusion attribution on the website: contributions + intercept equal the model's
    own log-odds to better than 1e-8 (checked again after v2's block weights were added).
  - Model bundles delivered are byte-identical (SHA-256) to what was installed.

19.7 A bug the corpus let us find in our own website
  DAIC-WOZ ships gaze in BOTH camera and head coordinates plus the head pose, so the
  website's conversion could be tested against ground truth. It was wrong: the code
  applied R^T . g where the corpus's own convention is R . g. Mean error 0.2304 vs
  0.0114 - a factor of 20. Fixed, and the fix is verified.

20. STAKEHOLDERS: WHO DOES WHAT
================================================================================

  PARTICIPANT (the person being screened)
     Gives informed consent (recording, automated analysis, storage, research reuse -
     each a separate choice). Speaks in the interview. Has the right to have their data
     erased. Never logs in. The report is written so it can be read aloud to them.

  COUNSELLOR (the primary user)
     Applies for access with professional credentials; enrols their own voice once;
     creates participants and sessions; uploads or records the conversation; reads the
     report; RECORDS THEIR OWN PROFESSIONAL ASSESSMENT, which is stored separately from
     the AI result and is the label used for future research; writes private notes.
     The counsellor - not the model - is the decision maker.

  COMPANION (family member or friend present in the room)
     Optionally enrols their voice at the start of a session so the system can exclude
     their speech from the analysis. This is what stops a parent's voice being scored as
     the participant's.

  ADMINISTRATOR
     Approves or rejects counsellor applications after checking documents; manages model
     versions (upload, validate, activate, revert); edits the enrolment passage; reviews
     which consented sessions enter the research dataset; exports the dataset; performs
     erasure requests; reads the audit log. Admin is a fully trusted role by design -
     uploading a model runs code on the ML server.

  RESEARCH / ENGINEERING TEAM (us)
     Trains models on the GPU machine, verifies parity with the website, packages
     bundles, and is responsible for the honesty of every reported number.

  GUIDE AND EVALUATION PANEL
     Assess the work. Everything in this document is reproducible from the repository and
     the results folders.

  (Not a stakeholder, but stated for completeness: the platform makes no autonomous
   decision about anyone. There is no automated referral, no alerting, and no action
   taken on a score.)

21. LIMITATIONS (stated plainly - a mentor will ask)
================================================================================

  L1  It is a SCREENING AID, not a diagnosis. In testing it is wrong about 3 times in 10.
  L2  Small data. 188 participants, a 47-person test set. A difference of a few points
      between models is inside the noise; our confidence intervals span 0.70-0.95.
  L3  Population mismatch. Training data is US adults interviewed by a virtual agent in
      English. Indian participants, accents, code-switching, and human interviewers are
      untested. Other languages are disabled until their own models exist.
  L4  Audio and video contribute little on this corpus. Forcing them to count (v2) costs
      accuracy. We ship it that way for multimodality, and say so.
  L5  Recording conditions change the measurements. Microphone level, camera, lighting
      and interview length can push features far outside the training range - we detect
      and display this, but we cannot correct it yet.
  L6  Train/deploy differences remain. DAIC-WOZ used an older face tracker (CLNF) and we
      run OpenFace 2.2; the corpus is segmented into utterance rows while our diarization
      produces speaker turns (we trained on turn-merged data to match, but it is not
      identical).
  L7  The PHQ-8 label is a questionnaire outcome, not a clinical diagnosis.
  L8  Short interviews are refused rather than scored. That is safe, but it does mean the
      system cannot help with brief conversations.
  L9  No automated test suite yet: verification is scripted parity checks, API tests and
      manual testing.
  L10 Not yet deployed beyond localhost; no HTTPS hosting, and the ML service must be
      kept off the public internet.
  L11 Single-language models today (English). Marathi/Hindi require their own training.

  A1  Honest evaluation end to end: official splits, one-shot test, repeated splits with
      selection redone inside each, bootstrap intervals, and published costs of every
      design choice.
  A2  Leak-free modelling: participant speech only, interviewer-dependent features
      removed, gender excluded - against a corpus where the published state of the art
      is partly an artefact of leakage.
  A3  Exact explainability, not approximation: SHAP in closed form, verified to 1e-15,
      down to individual sentences and face regions.
  A4  Refusals instead of silent failure: too-short, unidentifiable speaker, missing
      face, and out-of-range warnings on the report.
  A5  Operable without a developer: models are uploaded, validated by column name,
      activated and hot-reloaded from the admin screen.
  A6  Real speaker handling: voiceprint enrolment and diarization mean the right person
      is analysed, and the evidence for that decision is stored with the session.
  A7  Privacy by design: consent per action, recordings purged after feature extraction,
      erasure, audit trail, and features stored so future retraining never needs the
      original video.
  A8  A report written for two audiences: a clinician's breakdown and a participant's
      plain-language explanation on the same page.
  A9  Reproducible: every model bundle carries its own column list, threshold, training
      statistics and metrics; the website validates all of it on upload.

23. FUTURE SCOPE

  F1  Collect consented, counsellor-labelled sessions through the platform and retrain on

  F2  Calibrate for recording conditions - loudness normalisation, and per-device

  F9  Fairness auditing across gender, age and accent once enough local data exists.

24. QUESTION BANK - what a mentor or panel is likely to ask
================================================================================

--- THE PROJECT ---------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. Explain your project in one minute.
A.  A counsellor uploads a recording of a counselling session. Our ML service separates
    the participant's voice from everyone else's using voiceprints and diarization,
    transcribes the speech with Whisper, and measures three things: what was said (word
    habits and sentence meaning), how it was said (pitch, loudness, timing) and how the
    face moved (OpenFace action units and gaze). One early-fusion model trained on the
    official DAIC-WOZ corpus scores all 3,384 measurements together and produces a
    depression score against a stated cut-off. Every report explains itself with exact
    SHAP values, down to which sentences and which parts of the face moved the result.
    On the official test split it reaches AUC 0.846, accuracy 76.6%, and finds 12 of 14
    depressed participants.

Q2. What is novel about it compared with existing DAIC-WOZ work?
A.  Most published work stops at a notebook. We built a deployable, secured platform with
    real speaker identification, leak-free modelling, exact explanations at sentence and
    face-region level, admin-managed model versioning with name-based validation, refusal
    guards, and a consent-governed research dataset labelled by the counsellor rather
    than by our own model.

Q3. Who are the users?
A.  Admin and counsellor log in; participants and companions are recorded with consent.
    Section 20 lists each role's responsibilities.

Q4. Is this a medical device / can it diagnose?
A.  No. It is a screening aid. The report states that, gives the error rate, and stores
    the counsellor's own assessment separately. No action is taken automatically.

--- DATASET -------------------------------------------------------------------

Q5. Which dataset, and why that one?
A.  DAIC-WOZ (USC). It is the benchmark for depression detection from clinical
    interviews, used by the AVEC 2016/2017 challenges, and it has audio, transcripts and
    facial features with PHQ-8 labels for all participants.

Q6. How large is it, and how is it split?
A.  189 in the split files; 188 usable after excluding participant 440 (corrupt in the
    release). Official split: 107 train, 34 dev, 47 test, participant-disjoint.
    55 depressed (29.3%) and 133 not depressed.

Q7. How is the data labelled?
A.  PHQ8_Binary: the 8-item Patient Health Questionnaire scored 0-24 and thresholded at
    >= 10, the standard cut-off for probable depression.

Q8. Is the dataset balanced? How did you handle imbalance?
A.  No - about 30% depressed. We did not oversample. We report AUC, F1, sensitivity and
    specificity rather than accuracy alone, and we choose the decision threshold by
    Youden's J instead of 0.5.

Q9. Did you find problems in the dataset?
A.  Yes: participant 440 is corrupt; four participants are excluded by the release itself
    (342, 394, 398, 460); one participant's binary label disagrees with the score rule;
    three transcripts contain no interviewer rows; and the training split has an
    18-point gender gap in depression rate that does not exist in dev/test.

Q10. Why not use the interviewer's questions - they are part of the conversation?
A.  Because the interview branches on the answers, so the questions leak the outcome. A
    2024 ACL-published study reached 0.90 F1 using ONLY the interviewer's prompts. That
    signal will not exist in a real counselling session, so using it would inflate our
    numbers and fail in deployment.

Q11. Why did you use 80/20 when the corpus has an official split?
A.  We use both. The 80/20 split, repeated 10 times, is our primary estimate because it
    uses more data for validation and is far more stable. The official split is our
    secondary evaluation, because it is the only number comparable with published work.

--- FEATURES ------------------------------------------------------------------

Q12. What are the 3,096 text features?
A.  24 hand-counted lexical features (pronoun rates, absolutist words, negative and
    positive words, negations, hedges, past tense, sleep/fatigue words, answer lengths,
    vocabulary richness, ratios) plus 3,072 sentence-meaning features: each answer
    embedded by all-mpnet-base-v2 into 768 numbers, then pooled four ways - mean,
    length-weighted mean, max and standard deviation.

Q13. Why four poolings instead of just the mean?
A.  Each captures something different. The mean is the overall tone; the weighted mean
    lets longer answers count more; the max preserves the single most extreme statement,
    which an average would erase; the standard deviation measures how much the meaning
    varied across the interview.

Q14. What are the 64 audio features, and why not the original 85?
A.  Pitch (YIN, in semitones), loudness in dB, voiced ratio, jitter and shimmer proxies,
    answer durations, words per answer and speaking rate, each summarised by nine
    statistics. The original 85 included 24 features that depended on the interviewer's
    timing - response latency and between-turn gaps - which measure Ellie's scripted
    pacing rather than the participant. Removing them cost accuracy but is honest.

Q15. What are the 224 video features?
A.  14 facial action-unit intensities and 6 presence rates from OpenFace, their total,
    and 12 gaze directions - each summarised by mean, std, percentiles, max and
    frame-to-frame movement. Action units are muscle movements: AU04 is frowning, AU12
    smiling, AU45 blinking.

Q16. Why OpenFace and not a deep video model?
A.  DAIC-WOZ ships no raw video - only pre-extracted facial features - so there is
    nothing to train a deep video model on. OpenFace produces the same family of
    measurements as the corpus, which is what makes training and deployment match.

Q17. How do you ensure the features at deployment match the features at training?
A.  Three ways. The training run imports the website's own extraction functions; the
    bundle carries the exact list of column names, and the website builds each model's
    input BY NAME; and we verified on the corpus that the website reproduces the training
    features (lexical exact, timing to 1e-13) and reproduces the published AUCs exactly.

--- MODELS AND TRAINING -------------------------------------------------------

Q18. Which algorithm did you finally use?
A.  One early-fusion pipeline: per-modality median imputation, standardisation and PCA,
    block weighting, then L2-regularised logistic regression over the 40 components.

Q19. Why logistic regression and not deep learning?
A.  With 107-142 training people, deep models overfit. Earlier work on this corpus tried
    six deep and attention architectures - all lost to simple models. Linear models are
    also the reason our explanations can be exact rather than approximate.

Q20. What is PCA doing here?
A.  Reducing each modality to a small number of uncorrelated components (text 20, audio
    10, video 10). It prevents the 3,096 text columns from swamping 64 audio columns, and
    it keeps the total number of parameters small enough for 150 training people.

Q21. What is early fusion, in your own words?
A.  Joining all the measurements from all modalities into one vector and training ONE
    model on it, so the model can use relationships between modalities. Late fusion
    instead trains one model per modality and combines their output probabilities.

Q22. Why is naive early fusion a bad idea?
A.  Because of scale and width imbalance. We measured it: a global feature selection over
    all 3,384 columns chose 50 text columns and zero audio or video columns, so the
    "fusion" model returned byte-identical results to a text-only model.

Q23. How did you fix that?
A.  Per-block PCA so each modality contributes a comparable number of components,
    per-block weights (text 0.2236, audio 0.6325, video 0.3162) so no block wins by
    width, and an L2 penalty so no block can be deleted.

Q24. Why did v1 give audio zero weight?
A.  v1 used an elastic-net penalty, which contains L1 and can drive coefficients to
    exactly zero. It deleted the entire audio block. v2 uses L2 only, which shrinks but
    never deletes.

Q25. So does audio actually help?
A.  On this corpus, no. Dropping audio changes cross-validated AUC by +0.019 and dropping
    video by +0.051 - both slightly better without them. We include them because the
    project requires a multimodal decision, and we report the cost: -0.0577 AUC.

Q26. How is the decision threshold chosen?
A.  Youden's J - the threshold maximising sensitivity + specificity - 1 - computed on
    out-of-fold predictions of the training data only, never on the test set.

Q27. Why is the cut-off below 50%?
A.  Because only about 30% of the training participants are depressed, so the model's
    probabilities sit low. At 0.5 it would miss most depressed people.

Q28. What is out-of-fold prediction and why does it matter?
A.  A prediction made for a data point by a model that did not train on it. The
    late-fusion combiner is trained on out-of-fold probabilities so it never sees
    over-confident predictions, which would teach it the wrong weights.

Q29. How did you prevent overfitting?
A.  Strong regularisation (C=0.1, L2), dimensionality reduction, simple models, selection
    redone inside every cross-validation split, a test set touched once, and confidence
    intervals on everything.

Q30. How do you know your best model is not just a lucky split?
A.  Because we repeated the entire procedure on 10 different splits. One seed showed
    inner-CV 0.8796 but test AUC 0.5421 - proof that single-split numbers cannot be
    trusted at this sample size.

--- RESULTS -------------------------------------------------------------------

Q31. What accuracy do you get?
A.  On the official test split, the shipped model: accuracy 76.6%, AUC 0.8463, F1 0.6857,
    sensitivity 85.7%, specificity 72.7%. Repeated-split cross-validated AUC 0.7056 for
    the constrained model (0.7633 unconstrained).

Q32. Is that better than what you started with?
A.  Yes. The inherited baseline on the same protocol was AUC 0.768, F1 0.611, accuracy
    0.702. We improved all three.

Q33. Why is your accuracy lower than papers claiming 90-99%?
A.  Because those numbers mostly come from interviewer leakage, tuning on the test set,
    or unverifiable protocols. The one paper that reached 0.90 F1 did so by deliberately
    exploiting the interviewer bias - it was a warning, not a result to copy.

Q34. Which modality is most useful?
A.  Text, by a wide margin: 77% of the decision weight, and removing it collapses the
    model to chance (AUC 0.486).

Q35. What does ROC-AUC mean?
A.  The probability that the model scores a randomly chosen depressed person above a
    randomly chosen non-depressed person. 0.5 is guessing, 1.0 is perfect. It is
    independent of the threshold and of class balance.

Q36. Why report F1 and balanced accuracy rather than accuracy alone?
A.  Because 70% of the corpus is not depressed, so a model that always said "not
    depressed" would be 70% accurate and useless.

Q37. Your confidence intervals are wide. Why report them?
A.  Because with 47 test people they honestly are wide (roughly 0.72-0.95 for our AUC).
    Hiding that would imply a precision we do not have.

--- EXPLAINABLE AI ------------------------------------------------------------

Q38. What is SHAP?
A.  A method from cooperative game theory that splits a prediction among its features so
    that the parts add up to the whole. For a linear model it has a closed form:
    weight x (value - training mean) / training std.

Q39. Did you use the shap library?
A.  We compute the closed form directly - it is exact and needs no background sampling -
    and we cross-checked it against shap's LinearExplainer: agreement to 6.19e-16. We also
    verified additivity (contributions + intercept = the model's log-odds) to 3.55e-15.

Q40. How do you explain 3,072 embedding numbers to a counsellor?
A.  Two ways. They are summed into one item, "what was said, overall", which is exact for
    a linear model. And the text score is split across the participant's OWN sentences -
    exactly for three of the four pooling blocks, with the fourth reported as an
    unattributed remainder rather than being faked.

Q41. Why not Grad-CAM for the video part?
A.  Grad-CAM needs a convolutional network, spatial feature maps and the image itself. We
    have none: the video path is a linear model over 224 per-session statistics, and
    DAIC-WOZ ships no raw video to train a CNN on. Its gradient would be the model's
    constant coefficients - identical for every session, carrying no session-specific
    information. Presenting that as Grad-CAM would be decoration, not explanation.

Q42. So what do you show for video instead?
A.  A face-region attribution: every one of the 224 video features is mapped to the part
    of the face it measures, and the report shades a face diagram by each region's real
    SHAP contribution. It is labelled "which facial measurements the model weighed", never
    Grad-CAM.

Q43. What would make Grad-CAM possible later?
A.  A consented raw-video dataset, a CNN trained on face crops with enough participants,
    and evidence that such a model beats the action-unit features at all.

Q44. Can the counsellor see WHY a particular person was flagged?
A.  Yes: the strongest features with direction, the participant's own sentences that moved
    the score, the face regions that contributed, the modality shares, and a
    plain-language summary written to be read aloud.

--- SYSTEM AND ENGINEERING ----------------------------------------------------

Q45. Explain your architecture.
A.  Three tiers: React frontend; Spring Boot backend with PostgreSQL for accounts,
    sessions and reports; a FastAPI ML service for the pipeline and models. The frontend
    talks only to the backend with JWT; the backend calls the ML service over an internal
    API protected by a shared secret.

Q46. Why separate the ML service?
A.  Language fit, independent restart and scaling, and security: it is the process that
    loads .joblib files, and loading one executes code.

Q47. How does a model get updated without redeploying?
A.  The admin uploads a .joblib; the backend hashes and stores it and asks the ML service
    to validate it by column NAME; on activation a manifest is written and the ML service
    hot-reloads. The cache is swapped only after every model loads successfully.

Q48. How is the model upload secured?
A.  ADMIN role only, JWT-authenticated, the internal endpoint needs a shared secret
    compared in constant time, every file is SHA-256 hashed and audit-logged, size-capped
    at 520 MB, and the page warns that uploading a model runs its code.

Q49. What database design did you use and why Flyway?
A.  27 entities across accounts, clinical workflow, speakers, ML operations and
    governance. Flyway gives versioned, repeatable migrations (V1-V15), so every machine
    has the same schema and the change history lives in git.

Q50. How do you identify the participant in the recording?
A.  The counsellor (and any companion) enrol by reading a fixed passage, producing a
    256-dimensional voice embedding. Diarized speakers are matched by cosine similarity
    (>= 0.55 and a 0.06 margin); whoever is unmatched is the participant. If it is
    ambiguous the session is refused.

Q51. What happens if the ML service is down?
A.  The session is marked FAILED with a readable reason and can be retried; the rest of
    the application keeps working.

Q52. How did you test the system?
A.  Parity tests against the corpus (features and AUCs reproduced exactly), API
    integration tests of the model upload and activation rules, end-to-end pipeline runs
    on real recordings, unit checks of the explanation mathematics, and manual testing of
    every page and role. There is no automated unit-test suite yet - that is future work.

Q53. Give an example of a real bug you found and fixed.
A.  Four: (1) every short recording came back "depressed" because the text model returned
    a constant far outside its training range - fixed with the adequacy guard; (2) an
    explanation said "spoke loudly" when the value was below average, because direction
    was inferred from the contribution's sign - now read from the measurement; (3) the
    gaze conversion applied the rotation transposed, a 20x error caught against the
    corpus's own ground truth; (4) the report labelled the score "confidence", so
    "Depressed, 35%" looked contradictory - now labelled "depression score" with the
    cut-off drawn on the ring.

Q54. What privacy protections are in place?
A.  Consent recorded per action; recordings purged after features are extracted; features
    and transcripts retained instead of media; per-participant erasure; audit logging;
    role-based access; and the research dataset is opt-in and labelled by the counsellor.

Q55. What is stored after a session, and why?
A.  The feature vectors, the participant transcript, the report and its explanation, and
    the speaker-attribution evidence. That is exactly what a future retraining needs, and
    it is why the original video can be deleted safely.

--- BIGGER PICTURE ------------------------------------------------------------

Q56. What is the biggest weakness of this project?
A.  That the signal is almost entirely in the text, so the multimodal claim is weaker than
    it looks on this corpus - and that our training population (US adults, virtual
    interviewer) does not match our intended users.

Q57. How would you improve accuracy next?
A.  Not by another architecture on this corpus. By collecting consented, counsellor-
    labelled local sessions and retraining on them - that addresses population,
    recording conditions and segmentation together.

Q58. Can someone fake the result?
A.  Yes, as with any interview-based screening. It is designed for cooperative counselling
    sessions, and the counsellor's judgement is final.

Q59. What are the ethical risks?
A.  A false negative may delay help; a false positive may label someone wrongly. We
    mitigate by tuning for sensitivity, by stating the error rate on the report, by
    refusing unusable input, by never acting automatically, and by keeping the counsellor
    in charge.

Q60. Why should anyone trust your numbers?
A.  Because every one of them is reproducible from the repository: the split files, the
    protocol, the model bundle with its own metrics, the verification logs showing the
    website reproduces the training features and AUCs, and the decisions log recording
    every deviation and its cost.

25. GLOSSARY
================================================================================

  Action Unit (AU)     one facial muscle movement from the Facial Action Coding System
                       (AU04 frown, AU12 smile, AU45 blink)
  AUC / ROC-AUC        chance that a random depressed person scores above a random
                       non-depressed one; 0.5 = guessing
  Balanced accuracy    average of sensitivity and specificity
  Bootstrap CI         resampling the test people many times to see how much a metric
                       could vary
  Diarization          deciding who spoke when in a recording
  Early fusion         joining all modalities' features into one vector, one model
  Embedding            a vector of numbers representing meaning (sentences) or identity
                       (voices)
  F1                   harmonic mean of precision and recall for the depressed class
  Imputation           filling a missing measurement (here, with the training median)
  Late fusion          combining the output probabilities of per-modality models
  Log-odds             the linear model's raw output z; probability = 1/(1+e^-z)
  OOF (out-of-fold)    a prediction made by a model that did not train on that point
  PCA                  principal component analysis - compresses many correlated columns
                       into a few uncorrelated ones
  PHQ-8                8-item depression questionnaire; >= 10 means probable depression
  Precision            of those flagged depressed, the share who are
  Recall / sensitivity of the depressed people, the share flagged
  Regularisation       penalising large coefficients so a model generalises (L1 can zero
                       them out; L2 only shrinks)
  SHAP                 a feature's exact share of a prediction, summing to the prediction
  Specificity          of the non-depressed people, the share correctly cleared
  Standardisation      converting to (value - mean) / standard deviation
  Voiceprint           a 256-number summary of a voice, used to recognise a speaker
  Youden's J           sensitivity + specificity - 1; maximised to choose the threshold
